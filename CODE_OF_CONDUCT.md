# OpenRGD Code of Conduct

## Purpose

OpenRGD is an open technical project working on robot representation, interoperability and embodied systems.

The project welcomes disagreement, competing implementations and critical review. Participation must remain compatible with evidence-based engineering, personal safety and a professional environment in which contributors can challenge ideas without being attacked as people.

This Code of Conduct applies to official OpenRGD repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions and other spaces explicitly presented as OpenRGD community surfaces.

## Expected conduct

Participants are expected to:

- critique claims, designs, tests and evidence rather than the person presenting them;
- distinguish verified facts, inference, hypotheses and opinion;
- represent maturity, benchmark results, safety evidence and implementation status accurately;
- disclose relevant limitations, conflicts and uncertainty when making technical claims;
- respect privacy and do not publish another person's private information without permission;
- make room for contributors with different levels of experience and domain background;
- keep security and physical-safety disclosures inside the appropriate private channels;
- follow repository governance when proposing normative or safety-relevant changes;
- accept moderator requests to move, narrow or stop a discussion when it becomes unsafe or persistently unproductive.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following are not acceptable in OpenRGD community spaces:

- harassment, threats, intimidation or targeted personal abuse;
- discriminatory or demeaning conduct toward a person or group;
- unwanted sexual attention or sexualized conduct;
- doxxing, publishing private contact information or exposing secrets/credentials;
- deliberate impersonation or deceptive attribution;
- knowingly presenting candidate, experimental or historical material as accepted normative authority;
- knowingly presenting unverified measurements, vendor claims or simulated results as independent physical evidence;
- pressuring others to reproduce a security or actuation issue on unsafe live hardware;
- publishing exploit details contrary to `SECURITY.md`;
- sustained disruption of issues, reviews or discussions after a maintainer has asked for the behavior to stop.

Strong technical disagreement, negative test results and criticism of OpenRGD itself are not violations when expressed without the conduct above.

## Safety-sensitive discussions

Robotics discussions can cross from software into physical risk.

A contributor must not use an OpenRGD discussion, successful test, valid hash, model output or repository artifact as permission to operate hardware outside the relevant runtime, adapter, laboratory or safety authority.

When a discussion exposes a concrete vulnerability or physical-safety risk, follow `SECURITY.md` rather than continuing public disclosure.

## Reporting conduct concerns

Do not publish sensitive conduct reports or personal information in a public issue.

To request a private reporting channel, open a minimal issue containing only:

```text
[CONDUCT CONTACT REQUEST]
```

and a request for private contact. The current project steward will establish a private channel before receiving details.

If the concern involves the current project steward or a conflict that cannot be handled impartially inside the present single-maintainer structure, use GitHub's platform reporting/blocking mechanisms and preserve the relevant public evidence where safe to do so. The project should add an independent escalation path when additional maintainers are appointed.

## Enforcement

The current project steward is responsible for community moderation under the governance model in `GOVERNANCE.md`.

Depending on severity and recurrence, proportionate actions may include:

1. a private or public correction request;
2. a formal warning;
3. temporary limits on participation;
4. removal of content that violates this policy;
5. removal from project community spaces.

Security, threats, illegal content or platform abuse may also be escalated to the relevant hosting/platform provider or competent authority.

Enforcement decisions should record the concrete conduct at issue and avoid using moderation to suppress good-faith technical disagreement.

## Relationship to governance

This Code of Conduct governs participation. It does not change:

- normative specification authority;
- release or versioning rules;
- security disclosure requirements;
- physical execution authority;
- the maturity of any OpenRGD artifact.

Changes to technical authority continue to follow `GOVERNANCE.md`, `RELEASE_POLICY.md` and the repository's RFC/PR process.
